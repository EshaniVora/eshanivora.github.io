Who I Am

My background, what ties my experience together, and how this portfolio is organized.

Introduction

I am Eshani Vora, a student in the School of Arts and Sciences Honors College at Rutgers University – New Brunswick pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Exercise Science with a minor in Psychology, graduating in May 2027. My primary field of interest is currently orthopedic rehabilitation, and my long-term career aspiration is to earn my Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) degree and practice as an orthopedic physical therapist dedicated to restoring functional independence and providing patient-centered care.

Overarching thesis

My academic and clinical trajectory, spanning more than five years of healthcare volunteering, working as an outpatient aide, and shadowing in sports performance, demonstrates my progression from observing a clinic to becoming part of it.

Rather than viewing physical rehabilitation solely through a physical lens, my foundations in Exercise Science and Psychology demonstrate that recovery requires addressing both the injury and the patient’s mindset. Whether calming anxious patients after surgery or guiding them through progressive exercise protocols, my experiences show that technical competence has its greatest impact when paired with emotional intelligence and operational discipline.

Leadership & Solutions in Action

Three case studies showing how what I learned drove practical solutions on the job.

Teaching a patient their exercises

Physical Therapist Aide, Park Physical Therapy

My first learning outcome in the application of corrective exercise into daily clinical practice was seen when I worked to prevent incorrect movements while working as an aide at Park Physical Therapy. As patients transitioned from passive modalities, like their heating packs to warm them up beforehand, to their active exercise programs, some struggled to recall proper form for each exercise. As a result, they would perform exercises incorrectly and begin to overuse the surrounding muscles. Rather than simply reading off an exercise sheet, I used my knowledge to guide patients through each routine, demonstrating the exact movement first, offering verbal cues, and monitoring them throughout every set. Whenever a patient experienced unusual pain, I promptly alerted the supervising physical therapist, ensuring patients completed their sessions safely while giving therapists feedback to adjust their care effectively.

Draws on: Learned Outcome, Physical Therapist Aide

Supporting therapists with patients right after surgery

Physical Therapy Volunteer, Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center

Applying my second learning outcome in care after injury and clinical safety, I focused on helping with bedside mobility sessions to flow smoothly in the orthopedic unit at Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center. Patients recovering immediately after joint replacement or orthopedic surgery require closely supervised movement, meaning any obstacles, missing assistive devices, or setup delays can elevate fall risk. To support the clinical team, I took a proactive approach by preparing the treatment environment before each session, including sanitizing equipment, clearing a pathway in the patient’s rooms, and placing specialized chairs and walkers within immediate reach. Staying attentive during bedside transfers to assist therapists with equipment and patient stability eliminated setup delays, allowing the physical therapists to focus entirely on providing treatment to the patients.

Draws on: Learned Outcome, Volunteer

Gym equipment orientation at the senior center

Intern, New Brunswick Senior Citizen Resource Center

Drawing directly on my learning outcome in wellness in older populations, I addressed a common barrier among older adults at the New Brunswick Senior Citizen Resource Center who were hesitant to use the facility’s fitness equipment due to unfamiliarity with how to use the equipment or fear of injury. To overcome this challenge, I helped lead a gym equipment orientation for center members. During the session, I explained how basic strength and mobility routines protect everyday joint function, demonstrated proper safe-use protocols for each machine, and encouraged participants to ask questions in a welcoming environment so they would feel more inclined to use the available resources. By explaining the equipment and breaking movements down into approachable steps, I helped reduce uncertainty among members and gave them the confidence to exercise safely.

Draws on: Learned Outcome, Intern

Skill Matrix

A self-assessment of three soft skills and three hard skills, and where each one comes from.

Soft skills

Communication

Effective healthcare relies on translating complex clinical approaches into words that patients can understand. I built this skill over three years of volunteering at Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center in various positions, including the physical therapy department and the surgical reception desk. It is reinforced by my Psychology minor, which has shaped a communication style that prioritizes active listening and emotional validation. In my career as a physical therapist, this skill will directly strengthen patient trust, reducing anxiety that patients may experience during painful rehabilitation after injuries or surgeries.

Teamwork

Rehabilitation settings, such as hospitals, operate as tightly synchronized teams involving physical therapists, aides, nurses, and administrative staff. Whether anticipating therapist equipment needs during inpatient orthopedic mobility exercises or coordinating patient intake records across hospital departments, I consistently look two steps ahead to remove friction for my colleagues. This collaborative mindset ensures that clinical teams operate at peak efficiency even under high-volume pressure.

Problem-solving under pressure

No two patients respond the same way to a rehabilitation protocol. Working directly with many post-operative orthopedic and sports medicine populations during my time at Park Physical Therapy and Parabolic Performance and Rehab has allowed me to gain skills to assess unexpected fatigue, form breakdown, or clinic scheduling shifts in real time. Rather than strictly relying on a checklist, I can adapt to any unexpected situations, as each situation is unique in its own way.

Hard skills

Therapy exercise instruction

Through my hands-on experience as a Physical Therapist Aide, I have developed proficiency in guiding patients through resistance, mobility, and other exercises to support them through their recovery process. In addition, with my knowledge of human anatomy and muscles from my Exercise Science major, I can fully understand the actions and benefits of each exercise. I can accurately demonstrate movements, ensure proper posture, and monitor patients throughout to maximize benefits while preventing injuries.

Clinical intake and electronic documentation

Throughout my roles at Parabolic Performance and Rehab and Cooperman Barnabas Medical Center, I have developed proficiency in documentation, including check-in and check-out procedures and medical history updates. Accurately logging the patient’s daily exercises during sessions and other important information enables physical therapists to make adjustments to the patient care plans based on these updates.

Passive modality administration and clinical sterilization

I am experienced in preparing and administering various passive modalities, specifically therapeutic heat packs prior to sessions and ice packs post-treatment, while monitoring patient comfort. Additionally, I am able to help with organizing storage of extra materials, setting up treatment area sanitation, and breaking down patients after treatment.

Strategic 5 & 10-Year and Plan “B” Planning Model

Where I am headed after graduation, how I will get there, and what I will do if the biggest goal does not work out.

5-year career roadmap

  1. Finalize DPT program selection and graduation

    Having already earned acceptance to multiple Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) programs, my current goal is to select the program that best aligns with my career goals for the upcoming year. For the remainder of the school year, I will maintain a high GPA and complete all remaining requirements before graduation.

  2. Begin my DPT program

    Transitioning immediately from my undergraduate studies into learning the foundations of physical therapy, I will build advanced knowledge in anatomy, orthopedics, and manual therapy techniques. I will also leverage my undergraduate Psychology minor during early clinical labs to refine patient-centered motivational interviewing and pain-science education.

  3. Pass the NPTE licensure exam

    Near the end of my physical therapy program, I will focus on full-time clinical rotations in various inpatient and outpatient clinics to determine which type of therapy I am interested in pursuing after I graduate. Immediately after graduating, I will complete the National Physical Therapy Examination (NPTE) to get my state licensure.

  4. Begin practicing as a Doctor of Physical Therapy

    In my first year as a licensed DPT, I will join a practice in the New Jersey/New York area to apply the skills I have learned over the past few years and continue refining my clinical skills. Alongside treating patients, I will continue to study clinic operations for my future practice.

10-year macro-horizon plan, 2031 – 2036

  1. Specialization

    I plan to complete the required number of clinical practice hours to sit for the American Board of Physical Therapy Specialties examination in the field I intend to enter. Achieving board specialization will allow me and my future practice to stand out in a competitive field of professionals.

  2. Healthcare leadership and studying to open my clinic

    To prepare for my future goals, I will continue pursuing education in areas such as healthcare administration and finance while advancing my career toward becoming a Senior Physical Therapist. Managing clinic budgets, mentoring younger clinicians, and building relationships in the field will help lay the groundwork.

  3. Launching my own physical therapy clinic

    By 2036, my ultimate career goal is to open an independent physical therapy clinic. Designed around holistic patient care, my clinic integrates rehabilitation and mental health, bridging the gap in recovery after these difficult periods in the patient’s lives.

Plan B: academic and clinical education

If opening a clinic proves out of reach, my Plan “B” is to shift my long-term career to academic and clinical education.

  1. Become an APTA-credentialed clinical instructor

    Early in my outpatient career, I will complete the American Physical Therapy Association (APTA) Credentialed Clinical Instructor Program to supervise and mentor DPT students during their clinical rotations, building formal teaching experience directly in the clinic.

  2. Transition into student instruction

    Using my board certification and academic background, I will pursue professor positions at a college’s physical therapy program or undergraduate Exercise Science program, where I can do both clinical work and classroom instruction.

  3. Become a full-time academic faculty member

    I can enroll in further education, such as a Doctor of Education (EdD) or a PhD in Rehabilitation Sciences, while practicing, which can help prepare me to become a DPT faculty member and allow me to educate future physical therapists.
